# Participation Pool Engine v0.1

## What is implemented

Version 0.1 is a dependency-free C++20 kernel for analyzing one untranched
multi-project participation pool under an explicit finite joint distribution.
It is the first executable portfolio component of the common project financial
interface.

The engine does not infer independence, create a correlation matrix, resample
marginal project cases, calibrate probabilities, or call a larger pool safer by
assumption. The analyst supplies complete joint scenarios. Every scenario must
contain every configured project exactly once, so a shared technology, buyer,
input, regulatory, or funding shock appears directly in the paths it affects.

Inputs remain synthetic-only. The output is a physical-measure sensitivity,
not fair value, a market price, a rating, or a financing recommendation.

## Financial representation

Each project declares its development stage and maximum commitment. Within
each joint scenario it then supplies:

- dated capital draws;
- dated investor cash receipts, the identified source budget used by each
  receipt, and the part of each receipt that returns funded principal; and
- whether the claim is resolved or remains outstanding at the horizon.

The joint scenario—not each project separately—supplies the dated, identified
cash-source budgets available to all claims, together with its weight and any
common-factor tags. This matters when several projects rely on the same finite
guarantee, refinancing line, buyer, or support provider: their combined
receipts must fit inside one shared budget.

The implemented cash-source taxonomy is:

| Source | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `commercial` | Customer, offtake, capacity, or other operating cash |
| `licensing-royalty` | Licensing milestones, royalties, or technology-transfer cash |
| `exit-sale` | Cash from an equity, project, asset, or rights sale |
| `recovery` | Net distributable cash after a failed path |
| `refinancing` | Cash from a later financing claim; liquidity, not new underlying value |
| `explicit-support` | Cash from a named guarantee, public, philanthropic, insurance, or other outside provider |
| `sponsor-fee` | A financing fee paid by the sponsor rather than generated by the project |

This source record prevents a return from appearing without a payer. It also
keeps concessionary support and refinancing visible instead of silently
calling them operating value.

For project `i`, scenario `omega`, and month `t`:

```text
Investor cash flow_i,t(omega)
    = cash receipts_i,t(omega) - capital draws_i,t(omega)

Unreturned principal_i(omega)
    = total draws_i(omega) - principal actually returned_i(omega)

Principal loss_i(omega)
    = unreturned principal, if the path is resolved
    = 0, if the claim is still continuing

Outstanding principal_i(omega)
    = unreturned principal, if the claim is still continuing
    = 0, if the path is resolved
```

Pool costs are deducted separately. The pool path is:

```text
CF_pool,t(omega)
    = sum_i CF_i,t(omega) - pool costs_t(omega)

Loss_pool(omega)
    = sum_i Principal loss_i(omega)
```

Payment in kind is not a cash receipt. A contractual claim amount is not
treated as principal returned until cash is actually received.

## Enforced invariants

Validation rejects a configuration unless:

1. the model version and synthetic-input boundary are explicit;
2. project, scenario, factor, and layer identifiers are unique and safe;
3. positive joint-scenario weights sum to one within `1e-12`, after which the
   accepted near-one total is normalized and still reported;
4. every scenario contains every configured project exactly once, independent
   of input order;
5. all months and finite non-negative cash amounts remain within declared and
   resource limits;
6. cumulative draws never exceed commitment;
7. principal is never returned before, or above, funded principal;
8. receipts from all projects combined never exceed the cumulative budget of
   the identified cash source they reference;
9. unreturned principal on a continuing claim remains outstanding exposure
   and is not silently reported as realized impairment; and
10. project loss, pool loss, monthly cash, tail contributions, and any loss
   layers reconcile within scale-aware numerical tolerances.

These are financial conservation rules. They do not prove that an input cash
source, probability, contract, or recovery exists in the real world.

## Reported risk and return

The engine preserves every declared scenario and reports:

- total draw, largest same-month draw, largest same-month draw plus pool cost,
  and peak cumulative net cash outlay when funding settles before receipts;
- receipts and present value by external cash source;
- project and pool outstanding-principal and realized-loss distributions;
- project NPV before pool cost and pool NPV after cost at a declared physical
  hurdle;
- probability of principal impairment and probability of negative NPV;
- non-negative NPV-shortfall distributions;
- p50, p95, p99, maximum, ES95, and ES99;
- each project's contribution to the pool's ES95 and ES99 tail;
- pairwise weighted loss correlation, absent when either loss is constant; and
- explicit diversification benefit.

At tail level `alpha`:

```text
Diversification benefit_alpha
    = sum_i ES_alpha(Loss_i) - ES_alpha(Loss_pool)

Diversification ratio_alpha
    = 1 - ES_alpha(Loss_pool) / sum_i ES_alpha(Loss_i)
```

The ratio is absent when all standalone expected shortfalls are zero. Expected
pool loss remains the sum of project expected losses. Diversification changes
the distribution and concentration of loss; it does not erase loss or create
cash.

## What the deterministic tests show

Two fixtures hold each project's standalone binary loss distribution constant
and change only the joint outcomes.

In the **perfectly correlated** fixture, both projects lose 10 together or both
repay. The sum of standalone ES95 is 20 and pool ES95 is also 20:

```text
ES95 benefit = 20 - 20 = 0
benefit ratio = 0%
pairwise loss correlation = +1
```

In the **mutually exclusive** fixture, exactly one of the two projects loses
10 in each state. The marginal risk of each project is unchanged, but the pool
loss is always 10:

```text
ES95 benefit = 20 - 10 = 10
benefit ratio = 50%
pairwise loss correlation = -1
```

This is the central pooling result in human terms: two facilities with the same
standalone expected losses can produce very different pool tail risk. The
difference comes from when they fail together, not from how many names appear
in the vehicle.

A separate two-project hand table verifies dated draws, receipts, source
attribution, pool cost, NPV, project loss, pool loss, and loss-layer allocation.
Its adverse paths are intentionally uneconomic; passing a software identity is
not evidence of an attractive asset.

Additional regressions prove that a performing balloon remaining after the
horizon is exposure rather than loss; two projects cannot each consume the
same finite guarantee; the exact 95% probability boundary is stable; gross
same-month liquidity is not hidden by a receipt; project and record ordering
does not change published results; and declared dimensions cannot force
billions of empty project-month operations.

## Loss layers

Optional layers may be analyzed after the untranched pool. They must form one
contiguous partition from zero to aggregate contractual reference principal.
For attachment `A`, detachment `D`, and pool loss `L`:

```text
Layer loss(A,D) = min(max(L - A, 0), D - A)
```

Every scenario's layer losses must sum exactly to pool loss. The engine reports
layer expected loss, impairment probability, exhaustion probability, and loss
tails. It does not yet allocate pool cash yield among layers, solve a layer
spread, or call a lower-loss layer valuable. Priority reallocates loss; it does
not improve the projects. In legacy mode, reference principal equals project
commitment; in explicit-ledger mode, it is the declared contractual principal
limit, not investor purchase price or direct cost. The separate
[`CAPITAL_STACK_TERM_V0_1.md`](CAPITAL_STACK_TERM_V0_1.md) now allocates actual
principal and non-principal pool cash to a fully funded at-par stack while
leaving this untranched kernel unchanged; it still does not solve a market
spread or fair value.

## Current boundary and next integration

The implemented library surface is
[`portfolio.hpp`](../include/naturalehia/cellular_finance/portfolio.hpp), with
mechanics in [`portfolio.cpp`](../src/portfolio.cpp), the strict loader in
[`portfolio_config.cpp`](../src/portfolio_config.cpp), and deterministic tests
in [`portfolio_tests.cpp`](../tests/portfolio_tests.cpp) and
[`portfolio_config_tests.cpp`](../tests/portfolio_config_tests.cpp). The
reloadable format is specified in
[`PORTFOLIO_SCENARIO_FORMAT_V0_1.md`](PORTFOLIO_SCENARIO_FORMAT_V0_1.md), and
`naturalehia-participation-pool` prints the complete human-readable analysis.

The staged-capital adapter is now implemented in
[`portfolio_adapters.hpp`](../include/naturalehia/cellular_finance/portfolio_adapters.hpp)
and documented in
[`STAGED_CAPITAL_PORTFOLIO_ADAPTER_V0_1.md`](STAGED_CAPITAL_PORTFOLIO_ADAPTER_V0_1.md).
It preserves the actual configured facility cases rather than the separate
all-provider-performs fee sensitivity, and it requires completion repayments
to be allocated across real cash-source categories. Version 0.1 still does not
include empirical point calibration, an annual-engine path adapter, a project-
selection optimizer, a capital-call liquidity facility, dynamic/replenishing
waterfalls, or risk-neutral layer pricing. The annual engine currently exposes aggregate
summaries rather than stable pathwise cash flows, so those summaries must not
be combined as though they preserved dependence.

The separate
[`PROBABILITY_ENVELOPE_ENGINE_V0_1.md`](PROBABILITY_ENVELOPE_ENGINE_V0_1.md)
now computes exact financial ranges over bounded physical probabilities for
these fixed paths. It makes probability uncertainty measurable but does not
establish that the bounds or joint table are empirical.

The next implementation step is to expose stable pathwise annual-engine cash,
translate the remaining stage-specific instruments without discarding
dependence, and populate the probability bounds and stack terms from controlled
evidence. Only after calibrated untranched economics are credible should market
layer pricing or an investable legal form be researched.
