# Research Agenda

**Project:** Fostering Cellular Agriculture  
**Document status:** working research protocol, version 0.1  
**Evidence cut-off:** 27 August 2026

## Mandate

This project will investigate financial instruments that could make responsible
cellular-agriculture production capacity easier to finance and adopt. The work
begins with the financing failure, not with a preferred instrument. Debt,
equity, guarantees, insurance-like protection, offtake, advance purchase,
milestone finance, revenue participation, options, swaps, securitisation, and
public or philanthropic risk sharing are all candidates until evidence rules
them in or out.

The mission is to help displace animal suffering through safe and economically
durable production. That mission is a reason to do the work; it is not evidence
that any particular technology, facility, environmental claim, or financial
structure works. Underwriting conclusions must remain falsifiable, dated, and
separate from advocacy.

The initial reference problem is a **facility producing food from cultured
animal cells**. Broader cellular agriculture, including precision fermentation
and acellular products, may later require separate reference cases. In external
financial and regulatory work, use the terminology adopted by the relevant
authority or contract; do not treat “synthetic meat,” “cell-based food,”
“cell-cultured meat,” and “cultivated meat” as legally interchangeable.

## Core research question

> For a defined product, process, facility, jurisdiction, operator, and buyer,
> which risks prevent otherwise credible capacity from obtaining suitable
> capital, and what is the narrowest transparent instrument that places each
> risk with a willing party able to understand, price, control, or absorb it?

“Financeable” does not mean that a facility has an attractive press release or
a positive expected value in one base case. It means that identified providers
of capital can evaluate cash generation, downside, recovery, covenants,
counterparty exposure, and legal enforceability from evidence appropriate to
their mandate.

## Decisions the research must support

| Decision | Question to answer | Minimum decision evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reference asset | What exact production unit and cash-flow boundary are being financed? | Named jurisdiction, product specification, process, facility boundary, legal obligor, and intended use of funds |
| Technical readiness | Can the process repeatedly produce qualified output at the proposed scale? | Complete run-level history at relevant scale, including failures and downtime; methods and reconciliation checks; independent technical review |
| Completion risk | Can the plant be built, commissioned, and ramped within the proposed budget and schedule? | Engineering basis, scoped vendor quotations, critical path, contingency method, interface map, guarantees, and downside schedule/cost cases |
| Operating economics | What cash margin remains after all necessary inputs, losses, quality controls, and working capital? | Reconciled cost ledger and mass/energy balance; price and volume assumptions separated from contracted terms |
| Demand | Who will buy what, when, at what specification and price, and with what legal obligation? | Executed agreements and counterparty confirmation; non-binding statements clearly excluded from contracted revenue |
| Regulatory path | Which permissions, inspections, labels, and facility approvals are required, and what remains conditional? | Current primary legal and regulator records for the exact product, species, process, facility, and jurisdiction |
| Risk allocation | Who controls each risk, who bears it, and what happens after breach or failure? | Risk-allocation matrix, contract term sheets, credit support, settlement rules, termination events, and legal analysis |
| Instrument value | Does the structure add capacity or reduce financing friction rather than merely redistribute returns? | Counterfactual financing case and measured change in cost, tenor, probability of funding, commissioning date, or qualified capacity |
| Mission outcome | Is animal displacement credible and achieved without hidden safety or welfare trade-offs? | Defined counterfactual, cultivated share of product, animal-derived inputs, sales channel, and independently reviewable outcome method |

## Research principles

1. **Instrument-neutral until diagnosis.** A derivative is a possible result,
   not a requirement.
2. **Observed data before modeled performance.** Models organize evidence; they
   do not convert assumptions into observations.
3. **All runs, not selected runs.** Failed, aborted, contaminated, off-specification,
   and maintenance-affected runs remain in the dataset.
4. **No unlabelled extrapolation.** Scale, time, geography, product, and process
   transfers must be explicit.
5. **Regulatory scope is exact.** A consultation, safety opinion, inspection,
   label approval, or marketing authorization has only the scope stated by the
   issuing authority.
6. **Demand means obligation.** Surveys, memoranda of understanding, letters of
   intent, reservations, paid trials, purchase orders, and take-or-pay contracts
   are different evidence.
7. **Uncertainty survives the model.** Sparse or proprietary evidence widens
   ranges; it does not justify a precise point estimate.
8. **The downside is a first-class output.** Default, liquidity shortfall,
   completion failure, counterparty failure, recovery, and correlated stress
   matter alongside expected return.
9. **Claims are attributable.** Every material input carries a source,
   measurement boundary, date, owner, verification status, and transformation
   history.
10. **Safety and integrity are constraints.** No financial incentive may reward
    withholding adverse data, premature release, lower food-safety controls, or
    avoidable harm to workers or animals.

## Workstreams

### 1. Financing-failure diagnosis

Map the facility lifecycle from cell-line and process development through
engineering, construction, commissioning, qualification, ramp-up, stable
operation, and replication. For each stage, record:

- required capital, suitable capital type, tenor, and repayment source;
- risk owner, controller, bearer, and potential hedge counterparty;
- observable trigger and loss mechanism;
- existing contractual mitigants and residual exposure;
- why ordinary venture, corporate, equipment, or project finance is
  unavailable, too expensive, or mismatched; and
- whether the obstacle is risk allocation, missing evidence, poor underlying
  economics, or an actual absence of demand.

Interview both successful and rejecting capital providers. A lender's or
insurer's documented reason for declining a project may be more informative
than general expressions of interest.

### 2. Technical and operating performance

Acquire complete run-level records and establish distributions rather than a
single best case. Priority variables include:

- reactor working volume, mode, cell line and passage, inoculation, duration,
  harvest, cleaning, and turnaround;
- viable cell density, growth, gross harvested biomass, dry matter, qualified
  cultivated output, and finished-product composition;
- media, growth factors, gases, utilities, water, labour, consumables, and
  downstream losses;
- contamination, abort, rejection, rework, quality hold, and release outcomes;
- scheduled and unscheduled downtime, bottlenecks, maintenance, and seed-train
  dependencies; and
- differences between laboratory, pilot, demonstration, and proposed
  commercial configurations.

The [underwriting data standard](UNDERWRITING_DATA_STANDARD.md) defines the
minimum record structure. The initial literature establishes plausible
questions and model ranges, not bankable plant performance. For example,
[Humbird's peer-reviewed scale-up analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34101164/)
examines engineering and economic constraints through a techno-economic model;
it is not observed commercial operating history. A later
[scoping review of cultivated-meat techno-economic analyses](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39424999/)
documents how published studies rely on assumptions and analogies from adjacent
bioprocessing fields. Experimental papers on
[serum-free bovine-cell differentiation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-021-00419-1)
and [serum-free chicken-cell production](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00658-w)
are useful scientific evidence within their experimental boundaries, not
automatic proof of commercial yield, reliability, or cost.

### 3. Construction, commissioning, and supply chain

Build a reference design with a controlled scope and document:

- process-flow diagrams, equipment list, design capacity, redundancy, and
  utilities;
- engineering maturity and open design decisions;
- vendor quotations, quote dates and expiry, currency, taxes, freight,
  installation, commissioning, spares, escalation, and owner costs;
- schedule logic, permitting dependencies, long-lead items, interfaces, and
  contingency basis;
- vendor warranties, performance tests, liquidated damages, exclusions, and
  credit quality; and
- concentration and substitution risk for media ingredients, growth factors,
  single-use systems, sensors, and specialist equipment.

Claims about nameplate reactor volume or theoretical annual output must not be
used as proxies for qualified output or debt-service capacity.

### 4. Market demand and offtake

Segment buyers by product function rather than asking only whether consumers
like cultivated meat. Initial segments may include cultivated fat, biomass as
an ingredient in blended products, prepared foods, food-service applications,
and stand-alone products. For each target buyer, test:

- technical specification and acceptance protocol;
- annual minimum, maximum, and ramp volume;
- delivered price, indexation, floors, caps, rebates, and logistics;
- regulatory, label, certification, and launch conditions;
- take-or-pay, termination, rejection, force-majeure, and change-in-law terms;
- buyer credit quality, guarantees, collateral, and concentration; and
- willingness to prepay, reserve capacity, co-invest, or accept substitute
  product.

An independent buyer confirmation should identify whether an agreement is
binding and quantify remaining conditions. Customer surveys inform adoption
research but do not enter contracted-revenue calculations.

Advance market commitments are relevant as a demand-creation precedent, not a
template to copy. The
[World Bank's description of the pneumococcal-vaccine AMC](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/816001634659826556/pdf/Announcement-of-Funding-New-Vaccines-to-Save-Millions-of-Lives-on-April-3-2009.pdf)
shows a commitment to support future purchases at a predetermined price after
specified development and demand conditions. Research must determine who could
credibly fund, govern, and verify any analogous commitment here.

### 5. Regulatory and food-safety pathway

Create a jurisdiction-by-product approval map and obtain primary documents.
Regulatory evidence must distinguish product safety review from facility
inspection, process compliance, label approval, and permission to sell.

Initial authoritative anchors include:

- the US [FDA inventory of completed pre-market consultations](https://www.fda.gov/food/human-food-made-cultured-animal-cells/inventory-completed-pre-market-consultations-human-food-made-cultured-animal-cells)
  and USDA FSIS's [description of the joint oversight framework](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/inspection/compliance-guidance/labeling/labeling-policies/human-food-made-cultured-animal-cells);
- [FSIS Directive 7800.1](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/policy/fsis-directives/7800.1),
  which addresses inspection responsibilities for establishments harvesting or
  processing covered cell-cultured meat and poultry products;
- EU [Regulation (EU) 2015/2283](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32015R2283),
  which includes foods produced from animal cell or tissue culture within the
  novel-food definition, and EFSA's [novel-food guidance context](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/novel-food);
- Singapore Food Agency's [novel-food application guidance](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/regulatory-standards-frameworks-guidelines/novel-food-framework/guidelines-on-applying-for-pre-market-approval-for-a-novel-food)
  and safety-data requirements; and
- the joint FAO/WHO report on
  [food-safety aspects of cell-based food](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240070943),
  an authoritative hazard and regulatory overview rather than an approval of a
  particular process.

For every reference project, obtain an opinion from qualified local regulatory
counsel or specialists. Public summaries cannot resolve classification or
approval scope for a future facility.

### 6. Cash flow, credit, and valuation

Translate physical operations into an auditable cash-flow model. At minimum,
the model must produce revenue, operating cost, working capital, taxes, capital
spending, cash flow available for debt service (CFADS), debt-service coverage
ratio (DSCR), loan-life coverage ratio (LLCR), liquidity shortfall, probability
of default, loss given default, recovery, and instrument settlement under base
and adverse cases.

Use versioned machine-readable inputs and deterministic C or C++ computation
for the core risk engine. A reviewer must be able to reproduce every reported
number from source data and a tagged code version. Proprietary data may remain
in a controlled data room, but hidden calculations are not acceptable.

The research should be legible to regulated capital providers. The
[European Banking Authority's loan-origination and monitoring guidelines](https://www.eba.europa.eu/activities/single-rulebook/regulatory-activities/credit-risk/guidelines-loan-origination-and-monitoring)
provide an authoritative reference for prudent creditworthiness assessment,
monitoring, and adverse sensitivity analysis in the EU context. If an
instrument requires fair-value reporting, [IFRS 13](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-13-fair-value-measurement/)
is relevant: significant unobservable inputs and measurement uncertainty must
not be presented as though they were observable market prices. Accounting,
prudential, securities, commodity, insurance, tax, and insolvency treatment
will still require jurisdiction-specific analysis.

### 7. Instrument design and legal characterization

Only after the reference facility model is accepted should the project compare
candidate instruments. Each candidate must state:

- economic purpose and financing failure addressed;
- issuer, obligor, protection buyer/seller, beneficiaries, and eligible
  counterparties;
- notional, tenor, premium or return, reference variable, trigger, settlement,
  caps, floors, collateral, margin, reserves, and termination;
- data source, calculation agent, independent verifier, disputes, corrections,
  outages, contamination, redesign, and force majeure;
- basis, moral-hazard, adverse-selection, counterparty, liquidity, legal,
  accounting, tax, and regulatory risks;
- cash-flow waterfall and interaction with senior debt and covenants;
- losses in severe but plausible and reverse-stress scenarios; and
- evidence that the instrument improves the financing counterfactual.

Andrew Lo, José-María Fernández, and Roger Stein's
[research-backed obligation paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.2374)
is a methodological precedent: it links scientific-development risk,
portfolio behavior, and capital structure in a transparent simulation. It does
not establish that a portfolio or securitisation is appropriate for cellular
agriculture. Common process, supplier, regulatory, buyer, and technology risk
may make facility failures highly correlated.

### 8. Additionality, animal displacement, and safeguards

Define the mission outcome conservatively. Research must test:

- whether financed output displaces animal-derived output or merely adds a new
  premium category;
- the cultivated fraction in blended products and any animal-derived media,
  scaffolds, testing inputs, or continuing biopsy requirements;
- price, channel, geography, and likely buyer substitution;
- food safety, worker safety, and waste outcomes;
- facility-specific energy, material, water, land, and emissions boundaries;
- rebound effects and displaced production's response; and
- whether public or philanthropic support is proportional, capped, transparent,
  and additional.

Do not monetize “animals saved” or environmental benefits in underwriting
until the counterfactual, attribution, time horizon, and independent
verification method are agreed. Mission-linked payments should never depend on
unverifiable promotional estimates.

## Staged evidence-acquisition plan

### Stage 0 — Define the reference case

**Acquire:** product specification, process boundary, intended scale,
jurisdiction, facility owner/operator, buyer segment, funding need, and initial
risk map.  
**Produce:** definitions, source taxonomy, interview protocol, confidentiality
plan, and preliminary financing-failure hypothesis.  
**Gate:** no model is described as a facility forecast until all reference-case
fields are populated.

### Stage 1 — Build the public evidence base

**Acquire:** primary laws and regulator records; peer-reviewed experiments and
models; public planning documents; issuer filings; documented financing
precedents; and public contract announcements with their legal status.  
**Produce:** populated [evidence register](EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md), regulatory
path map, assumption inventory, and data-room request list.  
**Gate:** every quantitative input is tagged as observed, derived, modeled, or
extrapolated; material gaps remain visible.

### Stage 2 — Conduct confidential technical and commercial diligence

**Acquire:** complete run histories, quality and deviation logs, source records,
general-ledger extracts, invoices, engineering packages, vendor bids,
regulatory correspondence, customer contracts, and buyer confirmations.  
**Produce:** reconciled operating dataset, cost and mass-balance bridge,
construction estimate, contract matrix, and issue log.  
**Gate:** an independent technical reviewer can reproduce core performance
metrics and reconcile key cash costs. If evidence is sparse, uncertainty ranges
must widen and financing assumptions remain conservative.

### Stage 3 — Establish independent verification

**Acquire:** site observations, sampled source documents, calibration and meter
records, counterparty confirmations, legal opinions, and agreed-upon procedures
or assurance reports with explicit scope and exceptions.  
**Produce:** verification report, exception register, finalized data dictionary,
and frozen model-input release.  
**Gate:** no dataset is called “audited” or “assured” beyond the actual scope of
the practitioner's report.

### Stage 4 — Underwrite the reference facility

**Acquire:** capital structure, term sheets, tax and accounting advice,
insurance, collateral and security analysis, recovery evidence, and scenario
calibration.  
**Produce:** reproducible base case, downside cases, reverse stress, risk
allocation, central joint weights, evidence-supported probability bounds,
exact financial endpoint witnesses, a robust success-participation threshold
or explicit infeasibility result, a pooled-loss-protection threshold and
two-sided premium-feasibility result, and a provider price ladder separating
claim cost, collateral carry, capital charge, expenses, profit, investor
capacity, and catalytic support. For a reconciled pool, also produce an at-par
fully funded capital stack showing first-loss, intermediate, and senior cash,
loss, outstanding exposure, exhaustion, tail shortfall, NPV, prefunding drag,
and common-witness weighted-average life, plus a credit memorandum and
model-validation record. Use the
[calibration standard](PORTFOLIO_CALIBRATION_STANDARD_V0_1.md) and retain fixed
cash paths separately from the
[probability envelope](PROBABILITY_ENVELOPE_ENGINE_V0_1.md).  
**Gate:** the unassisted facility case is shown before any proposed support;
expected return is never reported without liquidity, loss, tail severity, and
probability-uncertainty results. If supported probabilities still admit
negative untranched NPV at the investor hurdle after the full evidenced success
cash right, the structure is not advanced to pricing without changed economics
or explicitly risk-bearing support. The term test is defined in
[Robust Success-Participation Term v0.1](SUCCESS_PARTICIPATION_TERM_V0_1.md).
Any proposed internal priority structure must then pass the cash-conserving
[Fully Funded Capital Stack v0.1](CAPITAL_STACK_TERM_V0_1.md): senior safety
may come only from funded subordination, not excluded losses, uncalled junior
capital, or reserve return mislabeled as project value.
Any proposed support must then identify a provider, cap, funding source,
performance assumption, trigger, settlement, counterparty exposure, and the
investor/provider price gap defined in
[Pooled Principal-Loss Protection Term v0.1](POOLED_LOSS_PROTECTION_TERM_V0_1.md).
Provider terms must then be reconciled through the
[Provider Price-Ladder Sensitivity v0.1](PROVIDER_PRICE_LADDER_V0_1.md) without
expensing capital or collateral principal, double counting funding and capital
cost, or calling a physical-probability floor fair value.
Provider performance must then be tested through the
[Provider Counterparty-Credit Stress v0.1](PROVIDER_CREDIT_STRESS_V0_1.md).
That calibration requires conditional default assumptions linked to complete
portfolio states, actual pledged-collateral terms, recovery and timing
evidence, and a named provider; seller default may reduce investor collection
but may never reduce the full-performance seller price.
The fixed claim must then be tested at rates admitted by the
[Market Observation and Hurdle Evidence Set v0.1](MARKET_OBSERVATION_AND_HURDLE_EVIDENCE_V0_1.md).
A promised yield cannot be applied to physical expected cash as though it were
the same return measure, and a rate derived from the target price cannot
validate that price. Disjoint comparable-consistent rate regions remain
disjoint when passed to the issue-price term.

### Stage 5 — Design and compare instruments

**Acquire:** counterparty appetite, legal characterization, hedge-accounting and
capital treatment where relevant, operational trigger feasibility, and pricing
feedback.  
**Produce:** two or three complete term sheets, valuation method, settlement
examples, counterparty exposure, abuse analysis, and counterfactual comparison.  
**Gate:** select only an instrument that addresses a demonstrated financing
failure, has verifiable triggers, and improves a defined outcome after all fees,
reserves, and risk transfer are included.

### Stage 6 — Limited pilot and monitoring

**Acquire:** live reporting, covenant tests, trigger data, settlement dry runs,
complaints and incidents, financing outcome, and mission outcome.  
**Produce:** monitoring dashboard, variance analysis, incident protocol,
independent post-pilot evaluation, and go/modify/stop recommendation.  
**Gate:** scale only after operational reporting and settlement work in
practice. Early pilots should have limited notional, qualified counterparties,
clear loss capacity, and no reliance on uninformed investors.

## Interview sequence

1. Process scientists, operators, quality leaders, and bioreactor engineers.
2. Engineering, procurement, construction, equipment, media, and utility
   providers.
3. Food manufacturers, food-service buyers, retailers, and institutional
   purchasers.
4. Food-safety authorities and regulatory specialists in the reference
   jurisdiction.
5. Venture, growth, equipment, project-finance, insurance, guarantee, and
   development-finance providers—including parties that declined projects.
6. Accounting, valuation, derivatives, securities, commodity, insurance,
   insolvency, and tax specialists.
7. Animal-welfare, worker-safety, food-access, consumer, and environmental
   experts.

Interview notes must distinguish fact, estimate, opinion, and confidential
claim. Interviewees receive no right to suppress adverse findings, although
legitimate confidential information may be aggregated or redacted.

## First deliverables

1. One-page reference-case definition.
2. Facility lifecycle and risk-allocation map.
3. Public evidence register and critical-gap list.
4. Underwriting data request and data dictionary.
5. Regulatory pathway memorandum for one jurisdiction and product.
6. Demand and offtake interview protocol.
7. Versioned physical-to-cash-flow model specification.
8. Independent-review and conflict-of-interest protocol.

No instrument should be named the “solution” before these deliverables expose
what is technically possible, contractually committed, and still unknown.
