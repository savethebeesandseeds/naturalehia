# Responsible Finance Charter

## Purpose

Fostering Cellular Agriculture exists to develop honest financial mechanisms
that can help safe, credible cellular-agriculture capacity reach useful scale.
Its moral purpose is to support a durable transition away from systems that
confine, harm, and slaughter animals.

Finance serves that purpose only when it funds real productive capability on
terms that institutions can evaluate and society can scrutinize. The project
will not use moral urgency to excuse weak evidence, conceal risk, inflate impact,
or transfer losses to parties unable to understand or bear them.

This charter applies to research, models, datasets, proposed term sheets,
partnerships, public statements, and any later pilot associated with the
project. Applicable law and independent professional duties take precedence.

## Governing commitments

### 1. Productive additionality

Every proposed mechanism must be tied to an identifiable productive activity:
research, demonstration, construction, commissioning, qualified output, or
replication. It must state how it is expected to improve capital availability,
financing cost, tenor, risk capacity, or completion probability relative to a
documented counterfactual.

Trading activity, fee income, transaction volume, or a higher valuation is not
by itself evidence of additionality.

### 2. Underwriting before advocacy

The project will begin with technical, commercial, and financial evidence. It
will not begin with a desired instrument and search for assumptions that make
it appear viable.

Animal-welfare purpose is a reason to do careful work, not a substitute for
cash flow. Projects with insufficient technical evidence should receive
research or demonstration finance, if appropriate, rather than debt presented
as infrastructure finance. Projects with negative private economics require
redesign or explicit concessionary support rather than hidden subsidy.

### 3. Claims no stronger than evidence

The project will distinguish:

- **proposal:** a contract or structure not yet subjected to complete review;
- **model result:** output from stated assumptions and code;
- **sensitivity:** a conditional result over chosen inputs without assigned
  probability;
- **stress test:** a deliberately adverse scenario, not a forecast;
- **estimate:** a value supported by a stated method and evidence basis;
- **validated performance:** repeated measured performance meeting predefined
  criteria under declared conditions;
- **financeable:** able to attract identified capital under stated terms, not
  merely modeled to do so;
- **bankable:** acceptable to identified lenders after diligence and approvals,
  not a synonym for promising;
- **hedge:** a position demonstrated to offset a defined exposure after basis,
  credit, collateral, and liquidity risk;
- **guaranteed:** supported by an enforceable obligation of a capable guarantor;
  and
- **animal-welfare impact:** a conservative, counterfactual-based estimate of
  reduced animal use or suffering, not gross production capacity.

“Low risk,” “market-ready,” “commercial scale,” “insured,” “diversified,” and
“investment grade” require a documented definition and evidence. Ratings and
regulated status must never be implied.

### 4. Full risk visibility

Every proposal must identify construction, technical, biological, operational,
food-safety, input, demand, price, regulatory, counterparty, liquidity, basis,
model, legal, correlation, and recovery risk where material.

Risk transfer must be shown from the perspective of all affected parties. A
risk has not disappeared because it is placed in a special-purpose entity,
excluded from a base case, moved off one balance sheet, or borne by a public or
philanthropic guarantor.

### 5. Risk with capable and accountable parties

Risk should first remain with or be allocated to the party best able to control,
observe, reduce, or absorb it. Sponsors, technology providers, contractors,
vendors, operators, buyers, lenders, insurers, guarantors, and public bodies
should not be assigned exposures solely because a structure permits it.

Sponsors and originators must retain meaningful exposure to the quality of
projects they select and information they provide. Guarantees and seniority
must not eliminate accountability for negligence, fraud, avoidable safety
failures, or misrepresentation.

### 6. Transparent subsidy

Public and philanthropic support can be legitimate where cellular agriculture
creates animal-welfare, environmental, food-security, knowledge, or other
benefits not captured in product revenue. Such support must be explicit.

For every grant, concessionary investment, guarantee, price floor, first-loss
position, tax benefit, or below-market service, disclose where measurable:

- provider and legal or institutional authority;
- policy objective and intended beneficiary;
- face amount, fair or expected value, and maximum exposure;
- fees, collateral, risk sharing, duration, and termination;
- expected private gains and public benefits;
- conditions, performance measures, and clawback rights; and
- losses, recoveries, and realized benefit over time.

The project will not describe returns created by undisclosed concessionary
support as evidence that an unsupported market exists.

### 7. Market purpose before speculation

Risk markets can improve investment when participants hedge real exposures and
counterparties can bear transferred loss. Speculation is not automatically
improper, but a new project-specific market must not depend on uninformed demand,
manufactured liquidity, or notional exposure detached from productive activity.

Early instruments should use documented eligibility, exposure-based position
limits, suitable counterparties, collateral and liquidity controls, conflict
management, and transaction reporting. Broad distribution or secondary trading
requires a separate market-integrity and investor-protection review.

### 8. Safety and welfare are not settlement variables to sacrifice

No financing covenant, milestone, or payment formula may encourage:

- release of product that fails applicable safety or quality requirements;
- concealment of contamination, failed batches, downtime, recalls, or adverse
  evidence;
- deferral of necessary maintenance, cleaning, testing, or worker protection;
- avoidable use or mistreatment of animals to meet volume, cost, or schedule;
- unreviewed biological or environmental release;
- misclassification of gross or unqualified material as saleable output; or
- retaliation against a person reporting a good-faith safety, welfare, legal,
  or financial concern.

Qualified output, not output at any cost, is the relevant economic quantity.
Food safety, worker safety, scientific integrity, and animal welfare are
constraints on the structure, not optional impact features.

### 9. Conservative animal-welfare accounting

The project will state the intended animal-welfare pathway clearly while
avoiding impact overclaiming. Analysis should identify:

- the animal-derived product and production system that may be displaced;
- the counterfactual without the financed activity;
- whether output is incremental and commercially substitutable;
- animal-derived inputs used by the cellular-agriculture process;
- adoption, rebound, price, and market-expansion effects;
- geography and time horizon; and
- uncertainty and possible adverse effects.

Installed litres, kilograms of gross biomass, or financing volume must not be
converted directly into “animals saved.” If a defensible displacement estimate
cannot be made, report capacity and say that animal-welfare impact is not yet
established.

### 10. Legal and institutional legibility

Research documents must use terms familiar to financial institutions and define
project-specific vocabulary. Economic substance, legal form, accounting,
taxation, regulatory perimeter, prudential capital, enforceability, insolvency,
consumer or investor protection, and public-budget treatment must be reviewed
separately.

The project will not choose a label to avoid a rule. A product that could be a
security, derivative, commodity interest, insurance contract, loan, collective
investment, or public liability must be reviewed by qualified specialists in
each relevant jurisdiction before solicitation, execution, or distribution.

### 11. Reproducibility and model governance

Quantitative work should be reproducible from versioned source code, inputs,
units, configuration, compiler, dependencies, and random seed where relevant.
C or C++ implementations should favor explicit formulas, deterministic tests,
plain data formats, and reviewable numerical methods.

Every material model must document:

- purpose, owner, version, and approval status;
- equations, cash-flow timing, units, and sign conventions;
- empirical inputs and provenance;
- assumptions without an empirical basis;
- deterministic scenarios before probabilistic results;
- distribution and dependence assumptions where simulation is used;
- calibration, validation, backtesting where possible, and limitations;
- numerical tolerances and failure behavior;
- independent review and unresolved findings; and
- changes to inputs, methods, or scope.

Uncalibrated ranges are stress ranges, not probability distributions. A precise
number produced by a model is not precise knowledge about the world.

### 12. Complete records and negative results

The project will preserve material assumptions, model versions, failed runs,
rejected structures, adverse diligence, review comments, conflicts, and changes
to acceptance criteria. Results must not be selected only because they support
financing.

Confidential commercial information may be protected through access controls,
aggregation, or an independent data trustee, but confidentiality must not be
used to make public claims that cannot be independently tested. Public outputs
should disclose the limits created by unavailable data.

## Minimum transaction record

Before any candidate advances beyond research, its record must include:

1. defined reference project and use of proceeds;
2. diagnosed financing failure;
3. baseline without the instrument;
4. complete sources and uses and contractual cash-flow waterfall;
5. counterparties, control relationships, and beneficial interests;
6. payment formula, triggers, limits, tenor, and termination;
7. risk allocation before and after the instrument;
8. expected, downside, and severe-but-plausible outcomes;
9. collateral, margin, liquidity, default, recovery, and workout terms;
10. fees, compensation, related-party payments, and conflicts;
11. explicit value and maximum amount of concessionary support;
12. legal, regulatory, accounting, tax, and prudential review status;
13. food-safety, worker, animal-welfare, environmental, and community review;
14. data sources, verification, model version, and limitations;
15. additionality and animal-welfare counterfactual; and
16. decision, dissent, conditions, responsible persons, and next review date.

## Governance and independence

### Roles that must be separable

Where proportionate to the work, the following functions should be performed or
reviewed independently:

- project origination and underwriting;
- technical and engineering verification;
- food-safety and quality verification;
- model development and model validation;
- benchmark administration and calculation;
- valuation and risk reporting;
- legal and regulatory review;
- animal-welfare impact assessment; and
- investment or pilot approval.

One organization may perform several functions during early research, but the
overlap and resulting limitation must be disclosed. A party whose payment
depends on a metric must not have unilateral control over that metric.

### Conflicts and compensation

Contributors must disclose financial interests, advisory roles, transaction
fees, ownership, funding sources, and material relationships with sponsors,
vendors, buyers, capital providers, and verifiers. Compensation should not
depend solely on transaction closing, modeled impact, or favorable validation.

Related-party terms must be identified and assessed against a defensible
arm's-length comparison.

### Review rights and dissent

Material technical, financial, legal, safety, and welfare objections must be
recorded and resolved or carried as open conditions. Reviewers must be able to
record dissent without having it converted into consensus language. No single
commercial sponsor should be able to suppress a material adverse finding.

## Go/no-go rules

A proposal is **no-go** in its present form if any of the following applies:

- the facility, product, use of proceeds, or qualified-output measure is not
  defined well enough to underwrite;
- material technical claims rely on selected best runs while failures or scale
  assumptions are unavailable;
- the cash-flow model does not reconcile or cannot reproduce stated results;
- the unsupported economics are negative and necessary concessionary value is
  hidden, denied, or presented as commercial return;
- the instrument does not address an evidenced financing failure or has no
  plausible additional effect on productive capacity;
- settlement is materially controlled by a payment beneficiary or cannot be
  independently verified;
- the proposed bearer cannot understand, price, collateralize, or survive the
  risk;
- leverage, margin calls, maturity, or refinancing can turn an operating
  setback into an avoidable insolvency without an adequate buffer;
- purported diversification depends on assets with undisclosed common-mode
  exposure;
- marketing would rely on retail or otherwise unsuitable investors bearing
  complex first-of-a-kind risk;
- the structure weakens safety, welfare, environmental, labor, scientific, or
  reporting standards;
- the animal-welfare claim lacks a stated counterfactual and is necessary to
  justify the transaction;
- a public guarantee or subsidy lacks authority, a cap, disclosure, monitoring,
  or a defensible public purpose;
- the structure relies principally on regulatory, accounting, or disclosure
  arbitrage;
- unresolved legal or regulatory classification could make the proposed offer
  or contract unlawful; or
- fraud, corruption, sanctions evasion, manipulation, material
  misrepresentation, or retaliation is suspected and not independently
  resolved.

A no-go decision is specific to the evidence and structure reviewed. It should
state whether remediation is possible and which conditions must change. Moral
importance is not grounds to waive a gate.

## Conditions for a limited pilot

A research pilot may proceed only when:

- the instrument and eligible counterparties are narrowly scoped;
- no public solicitation or broad secondary trading occurs;
- each party receives clear risk, fee, conflict, and liquidity disclosure;
- maximum loss and notional exposure are capped;
- collateral and default procedures are operationally tested;
- reference data are independently verifiable;
- the baseline and success measures are fixed in advance;
- legal and regulatory reviews authorize the actual pilot terms;
- safety and welfare protections cannot be waived by financial covenant; and
- an orderly termination and complaint process exists.

Pilot success must be evaluated by financing additionality, hedge effectiveness
or risk reduction, operational reliability, total cost, adverse incentives, and
mission contribution—not merely by execution or investor demand.

## Failure, workout, and remedy

Responsible finance includes responsible failure. Structures should define:

- early-warning metrics and reporting frequency;
- cure periods and capital-call rules;
- payment priority during stress;
- continuation of payroll, safety, quality, animal-care, and environmental
  obligations;
- lender or substitute-operator step-in subject to competence and law;
- restructuring principles and treatment of concessionary providers;
- data preservation and incident reporting;
- equipment disposition, site remediation, and decommissioning; and
- investigation, recovery, and remedy for misconduct or preventable harm.

Financial creditors must not extract value in a manner that leaves unfunded
safety, animal-care, employment-law, or remediation obligations.

## Public communication

Public materials must separate aspiration, modeled result, contractual
commitment, measured performance, and realized impact. They should disclose
material uncertainty, conflicts, concessionary support, and whether an
instrument has received legal, regulatory, investment, or credit approval.

The project will correct material errors promptly and retain an accessible
record of substantive corrections. It will not use images or accounts of animal
suffering to pressure a person into an unsuitable investment or to prevent
legitimate scrutiny of project economics.

## Revision and accountability

This charter is an operating constraint, not promotional language. A material
change should identify the evidence, incident, legal development, or governance
need that motivated it. Earlier versions and the conditions under which prior
decisions were made should remain available.

If a proposed transaction cannot satisfy this charter, the project should
redesign it, narrow it, return it to research, or stop. Declining to finance an
unsupported structure protects both the credibility of cellular agriculture
and the long-term effort to reduce animal suffering.
