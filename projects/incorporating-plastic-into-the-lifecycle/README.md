# Incorporating Plastic into the Lifecycle

**Incorporating Plastic into the Lifecycle is a Naturalehia project idea
investigating whether plastic-derived matter and chemical energy can be
converted into living biomass and passed through a food chain.**

> **Project status: untested research idea.** This project has not demonstrated
> such a lifecycle. Nothing produced by it should be considered safe food for
> people, livestock, or wildlife.

## Core idea

A bacterium or microbial community breaks down a defined plastic and uses some
of its products as a source of carbon and chemical energy. The microorganisms
turn part of that material into their own biomass. A suitable organism then
eats the microorganisms, another organism eats that organism, and the process
continues through successive trophic levels.

The goal is to demonstrate that carbon originating in plastic can be
biologically assimilated and measurably incorporated into a contained living
cycle. Making plastic disappear or fragment into smaller pieces would not be
enough; the project would need to show real uptake and transfer between
organisms.

## What “turn plastic into food” means

Here, **food** means biomass that can nourish the next organism in a controlled
experimental food chain. It does not mean feeding plastic directly to animals,
and it does not imply that the resulting biomass is fit for human or animal
consumption. Any credible demonstration must trace the plastic-derived carbon,
account for energy and material losses, and test for residual plastic,
additives, toxic by-products, pathogens, and bioaccumulation.

## Possible first demonstration

1. Select one well-characterized plastic and a microorganism capable of both
   degrading and assimilating material derived from it.
2. Grow it in a contained system with appropriate no-plastic and no-microbe
   controls.
3. Verify that plastic-derived carbon enters microbial biomass rather than
   merely being fragmented, ideally with a traceable label and a mass balance.
4. Test whether an appropriate second organism can consume that biomass, using
   an ethically and scientifically reviewed protocol.
5. Measure transfer to each later trophic level, together with losses,
   contaminants, health effects, and nutritional value.

## Questions for a future project definition

- Which plastic-and-microorganism pair is the safest and clearest starting
  point?
- How much plastic-derived carbon becomes biomass rather than emissions,
  waste, or unusable by-products?
- Can that biomass nourish the next trophic level without transferring or
  concentrating harmful substances?
- How many trophic steps remain meaningful once biological energy losses are
  measured?
- What evidence would justify saying that plastic has entered a lifecycle
  rather than simply undergone degradation?
